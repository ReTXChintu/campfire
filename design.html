Foundations

Campfire's existing dark theme stays, but it's under-specified today. These are the tokens every screen below is built from — a warm, low-saturation dark palette (not a cold near-black), one accent reserved for meaning rather than decoration, and elevation expressed through surface tone and blur rather than drop shadows, since panels genuinely sit on top of moving video.

Ember marks anything actionable or "live" (play button, start-party CTA, active-speaker ring). Gold is reserved for one job only: the TV focus ring, so focus never gets confused with a normal accent. The locked state uses a muted red hairline, not the whole banner in red — it's information, not a scolding.

Components

The existing controls (play/pause, ±10s, scrub, speed, quality, subtitles, episode list, Skip Intro / Next Episode, back+title) keep their current behavior — they're re-skinned onto these tokens, not redesigned. What's new is the Watch Party layer: camera tile, roster row, the lock banner, join/control toasts, and the party code chip.

The full journey

Login to logout, mapped once for every platform. Screens in white get a full mockup further down because they're where the hard, platform-specific problems live. Screens in grey follow ordinary streaming-app conventions (a profile picker, a title page, an account screen) and are called out with layout notes rather than pixel mockups — happy to go deeper on any one of them.

TV skips the profile-picker step's typing entirely — see the login mockup below. Mobile's "Home" is portrait; the moment a video starts, mobile rotates to the landscape player. Desktop/web share this exact map; the only structural difference is windowed vs. browser-tab chrome, noted per-screen below.

TV focus flow

The one truly new input problem: D-pad only, no pointer, no keyboard. Two focus "modes" — Watching (chrome hidden, party rail closed) and Controlling (chrome visible, focus lives in a control row or the party rail). Every path back to Watching is a single Back press away, and there's no control that can trap focus.

Why a rail instead of a modal: a modal chat window would steal focus from the video state entirely and risk a dead end if it doesn't map Back correctly. A docked rail keeps "you're still in the player" legible, and D-pad Left always walks focus back out of it toward the control row, never off the edge of the screen.

Android TV

10-foot legibility, D-pad only, and the one hard rule: TVs never show camera video. A TV device shows up in the roster with a device icon, never as a black or placeholder tile.

Login — pairing, not typing

TV login — pairing code (no on-screen keyboard needed)

Password entry with a D-pad is painful and a known source of TV-app abandonment — Campfire pairs the TV to an already-logged-in phone/browser session instead, the same pattern YouTube and most streaming TV apps use. An on-screen keyboard remains as a fallback only.

Windows desktop & web

Mouse + keyboard, a resizable window (or browser tab), and actual room to spare — so the party panel shrinks the video into a letterboxed area instead of floating over it. Web and the desktop app share this exact layout; the only difference is web requests the Fullscreen API instead of an OS-level window, and has no window-chrome to manage.

Joins & control changes

One pattern everywhere: a transient toast in a top corner, never the bottom (where playback controls live), auto-dismissing, never stealing input focus — so a TV user doesn't lose their place mid-navigation just because someone joined.

EventPlacementDurationNotes
Someone joinedTop-right (top-center on TV)~4sNeutral border, avatar + name, stacks up to 2 with a "+N more"
You were granted controlSame corner~6s or dismissEmber accent border — it changes what you can do, so it earns a beat longer on screen
Your control was revokedSame corner~6s or dismissPaired with the lock banner appearing in chrome — the toast explains the moment, the banner explains the ongoing state
TV: any of the aboveTop-center, outside the focus row~5sNever receives D-pad focus itself — purely informational

Where we're cutting scope

Cramming every feature onto every platform is how the current app got messy. These cuts are deliberate.

✕
No camera video on Android TV — ever

TVs are represented as a device in the roster with a small "TV" badge, the same place a camera-off phone shows up. Building even a disabled camera affordance invites someone to ask why it doesn't work; not building it avoids the question.

✕
No tile for camera-off participants, on any platform

A tile only appears once a camera turns on. Camera-off people live in the roster, not as an empty rectangle taking up strip space next to the movie.

✕
No full-screen camera grid, anywhere

Campfire is video-first, not a video-call app with a movie playing in the corner. Camera tiles stay a small strip; a full Zoom-style grid would invert that.

✕
Typed chat is not the primary TV input

An on-screen keyboard over D-pad is slow enough that it discourages use. Quick reactions are the default; full typed chat stays available as a secondary path (or via phone-as-remote pairing), not the first thing offered.

✕
Companion mode gets no dedicated screens yet

A second device joining for chat/calling only is the mobile portrait chat+roster layout with the video slot removed. If the layout is componentized as designed here, this mode falls out for free later rather than needing its own design pass now.
